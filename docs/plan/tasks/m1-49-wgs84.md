# M1-49 — The WGS-84 ellipsoid

Phase: C | Status: not started
Prerequisites: M1-09

## Purpose

Earth is not a sphere, and the milestone plan says so: *"WGS-84 ellipsoid, not a
sphere — Orbiter models the flattening and so should this."* The polar radius is
21.4 km shorter than the equatorial one, which is 0.3 % and is visible on the
limb; more importantly, everything placed on the surface from here on — tiles,
elevation, ground tracks, launch sites — is placed on this shape.

The trap this task exists to avoid is the **geodetic versus geocentric latitude**
distinction. They differ by up to 11.5 arcminutes at mid-latitudes, which is
about 21 km on the ground, and confusing them produces results that are wrong in
a way that looks entirely plausible.

## What to implement

`src/view/Ellipsoid.hpp` / `.cpp`.

- The WGS-84 defining constants with their source: semi-major axis
  `a = 6378137.0 m` exactly, and flattening `1/f = 298.257223563` exactly. The
  semi-minor axis and eccentricity are **derived**, not typed in, so they cannot
  disagree.
- `geodeticToCartesian(Radians latitude, Radians longitude, Metres height)`, and
  the inverse by a **closed-form method** — Bowring's or Fukushima's — rather
  than an iteration. Guideline: prefer the formulation with no loop to the loop
  that has to report non-convergence, when one exists.
- `geodeticSurfaceNormal(...)`, which is **not** the normalised position vector.
  The two differ by exactly the geodetic-geocentric latitude difference, and
  every lighting calculation on the surface needs the geodetic one.
- `geocentricFromGeodetic` and back, as named functions, so a conversion is
  never implicit.
- Radius as a function of geodetic latitude, and the two special values as named
  constants for tests to lean on.

## Out of scope

Geoid undulation — the difference between the ellipsoid and mean sea level, up
to about 100 m. Elevation is referenced to the ellipsoid throughout this
milestone and that is written down here, because it is exactly the sort of
offset that gets discovered later as a mystery.

Map projections beyond the equirectangular one the tiles already use.

## Tests

`tests/test_ellipsoid.cpp`, headless.

- **Round trip** geodetic → Cartesian → geodetic to **1e-9 m** in height and
  1e-12 rad in angle, over a seeded sweep of latitudes, longitudes and heights
  from −11 km to +36,000 km — the last because a camera in geostationary orbit
  is a legitimate input.
- **The singularities**, per rule 5: both poles exactly, the equator exactly,
  longitude ±180°, height exactly zero, and a point at the centre of the Earth,
  which is reported rather than returning NaN.
- **Against published values**: the geodetic-geocentric latitude difference
  peaks at **11.5 arcminutes near 45°**, and the polar radius is
  **6,356,752.314 m** — both independently published numbers that a wrong
  flattening or a confused latitude fails.
- **The normal is not the position**: at 45° latitude the angle between the
  geodetic normal and the geocentric direction equals that published difference,
  within 1e-9 rad. This is the assertion that stops the whole class of bug.
- **Flattening is visible**: the equatorial minus polar radius is 21,384.7 m,
  checked against the derived constants.
